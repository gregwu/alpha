Momentum

Instead of just RSI:

Calculate:

* 5-day return
* 10-day return
* 20-day return
* 60-day return
* 120-day return
* 250-day return

Research shows different lookback windows capture different behaviors.

Relative Strength

One of the most important factors.

For each stock:

Return(stock) - Return(SPY)

Do this for:

* 5 days
* 20 days
* 60 days
* 120 days

Volume Factors


Compute:

* Volume percentile
* Relative volume
* Dollar volume
* OBV slope
* VWAP distance
* Accumulation score

Large institutions leave footprints in volume before price trends become obvious.

⸻

Volatility

Calculate:

* ATR
* Historical volatility
* Parkinson volatility
* Bollinger Band width
* Volatility contraction
* Volatility expansion

The transition from low to expanding volatility often precedes large moves.

Layer 3 — Market Regime

Do not trade aggressively all the time.

Classify the market:

* Bull
* Neutral
* Bear
* High volatility
* Panic

Features:

* SPY > 200-day average
* VIX level
* Advance/decline line
* New highs versus new lows
* Breadth indicators

The same stock score should be interpreted differently depending on the market regime.

⸻

Layer 4 — Composite Score

Normalize each factor to a z-score.

For example:

Factor	Weight
Relative strength	25%
Trend	20%
Volume	15%
Fundamentals	15%
Volatility	10%
ICT structure	10%
Options	5%

Start with equal or research-backed weights, then let optimization refine them.

⸻

Layer 5 — Machine Learning

This is where many people go wrong.

Don’t ask the model:

“Will this stock go up?”

Instead ask:

“Among all tradable stocks today, which are most likely to outperform the others over the next 5 or 20 trading days?”

That’s a ranking problem rather than a binary prediction.

Algorithms worth testing:

* XGBoost

These often outperform deep learning on structured tabular financial data.

⸻

Layer 6 — Portfolio Construction

This is an area where many retail systems underperform.

Rather than buying every stock above a threshold:

1. Rank all stocks.
2. Buy the top 30.
3. Equal-weight them (or use volatility-adjusted weights).
4. Limit sector exposure (for example, no more than 20% in one sector).
5. Cap individual positions (for example, 5% each).

The portfolio construction process matters as much as the signal.

⸻

Layer 7 — Risk Management

Separate the decision to buy from the decision to size.

Ideas:

* Maximum portfolio volatility target.
* Maximum position size.
* Sector concentration limits.
* Maximum portfolio drawdown.
* Dynamic cash allocation when the market regime deteriorates.

Avoid optimizing only for return; optimize for risk-adjusted performance.

⸻

Layer 8 — Feedback Loop

Every month:

* Retrain the model using only historical data available at that time.
* Evaluate factor performance by market regime.
* Remove factors that have become ineffective.
* Introduce new candidate factors and keep only those that add incremental value in out-of-sample testing.

Treat factors as hypotheses that must continually earn their place.

What I would build for your specific environment

You already have a strong foundation. I’d organize it into four independent models that feed one master ranking engine:

Model	Purpose
Market Regime Model	Bull, neutral, bear, high-volatility classification
Stock Ranking Model	Predict relative performance over the next 5–20 trading days
Entry Timing Model	Decide whether to enter now or wait for a pullback, using intraday features such as VWAP, volume, and your ICT-derived metrics
Position Management Model	Optimize sizing and exit decisions based on evolving risk and portfolio constraints

This separation makes each component easier to evaluate and improve.

One enhancement I think could become your differentiator

Most retail quantitative systems ignore cross-sectional relationships. Instead of scoring each stock independently, build a daily graph where:

* Nodes represent stocks.
* Edges represent relationships such as return correlation, sector membership, supply-chain links, or common institutional ownership.

Add graph-derived features (for example, neighbor momentum, sector propagation, and centrality) to each stock’s feature set. A graph-based layer can capture information that isolated indicators miss.

⸻

If we were building this as a serious research platform rather than a single trading strategy, I’d aim for a system with roughly:

* 300–500 engineered features per stock,
* 2,000–5,000 tradable U.S. stocks,
* 10–15 years of daily history,
* walk-forward retraining every month,
* cross-sectional ranking instead of directional prediction,
* and rolling factor attribution so you can see which signals are driving performance in each market regime.

That would be much closer in philosophy to institutional quantitative investing than simply optimizing entries and exits on individual charts.
